# Original text written for this baseline, Apache-2.0 like the rest of the repository. Each
# "=== name" line opens a page; every other non-empty line is one block (a paragraph), passed
# to the engine in order. Lines before the first page are comments.

# What the pages exercise: news prose with figures, dialogue and contractions, phrasal verbs
# and idioms, technical text with code, URLs and versions, academic register, a recipe,
# informal speech, homographs, a mixed-language page (French and Spanish blocks, a heading, a
# number, a two-letter block), an archaic register, and tense and clitic grammar. The words

# risk once a second language exists.

=== technical
To install the command-line tool, run npm install --global lingua-cli and restart your terminal. The configuration lives in ~/.config/lingua/settings.json.
If the request fails with HTTP 503, the client retries up to three times with exponential back-off. Version 2.4.1 fixed a bug where the cache wasn't invalidated after a schema change.
See https://example.com/docs/getting-started for details, or email support@example.com. State-of-the-art models still struggle with long, poorly structured documents.

=== academic
Recent studies suggest that adults acquire vocabulary more efficiently when new words are encountered repeatedly in meaningful contexts, rather than memorised from isolated lists (e.g., flashcards without sentences).
However, the evidence is far from conclusive. Smith et al. found that the benefits of extensive reading were greatest among intermediate learners, whereas beginners showed little measurable improvement.
These findings were subsequently replicated, although the effect sizes reported in later work were considerably smaller than those in the original experiments.
