## 1. French's pre-pass (crates/lingua-core)

- [x] 1.1 `analysis/tokenize.rs`: French takes an arm of its own in `tokenize`, the language-neutral rules kept (segmentation, the one-`-` hyphen run, the digit drop, the edge-apostrophe trim, the single-letter rule); English's and Spanish's paths are not touched. First rule: a French word is cut at every U+202F, each part keeping its own byte span (design D2). Unit tests: « «\u{202F}C’est fini\u{202F}! » » gives `Ce`, `est`, `fini` with no space in any token or span; U+00A0 needs nothing; English and Spanish text holding U+202F tokenises as before.
- [x] 1.2 Elision (D3): the table of elided forms and the words they stand for, as one sorted constant with a doc line per entry; `'` and `’`; a following letter required; `si` before `il`/`ils`; `moi`/`toi` right after a hyphen; the read word's own first letter takes the written capital; the rule repeated on the remainder; the elided piece's span ends after its apostrophe (three bytes for `’`); an elided form followed by its apostrophe and by neither a letter nor a digit is read on its own. Unit tests: every entry, capitalised and not, straight and typographic; `L'homme` and `l’horizon` with their byte spans; `jusqu'au`, `qu'aujourd'hui`; `aujourd'hui`, `presqu'île`, `quelqu'un`, `prud'homme`, `entr'ouvert`, `grand'mère` whole; `lʼhomme` (U+02BC) whole; `l’` alone and `l’ homme`; `d'1` gives no token (a digit is no letter, and `d` alone falls to the single-letter rule).
- [x] 1.3 `au`/`aux` (D4): `à` + `le`/`les` sharing the span; `du`, `des`, `auquel`, `auxquels`, `duquel`, `desquels` whole; nothing split inside a compound (`au-dessus`). The capital is put on the read word's own first letter — the shared casing code slices the base at the written letter's byte length and must not be reused for `à`. Unit tests: `Au`, `AU`, `AUX`, `jusqu'au`, `au-delà`.
- [x] 1.4 Hyphenated runs (D5), in order: a digit → each piece on its own; the whole run listed → one token; an elision on the first piece split off, the rest read again; every later piece a lowercase pronoun of the list (the elided `m'`/`t'`/`l'` before `en`/`y` included, the euphonic `t` only before `il`, `elle`, `on`, `ils`, `elles`) → each piece a token with its own span, no token for `t`; otherwise the compound rule. Unit tests: `dit-il`, `a-t-il`, `pense-t-elle`, `Viendront-ils`, `Qu’est-ce`, `Est-ce`, `Va-t'en`, `Donne-m'en`, `allez-vous-en`, `coupez-les`, `dis-le-moi`, `Vas-y`, `Prends-en` split with their spans; `rendez-vous` and `peut-être` whole when listed; `l'arc-en-ciel` → `le` + `arc-en-ciel`; `c'est-à-dire` whole when listed; `celui-ci`, `celle-là`, `moi-même`, `Jean-Pierre`, `Saint-Y`, `DIT-IL` follow the compound rule.
- [x] 1.5 `analysis/mod.rs`: `FRENCH_ANALYZER_VERSION = "0.2.0"`, its doc saying what `0.2.0` adds (D9). Doc comments: `Token` (the halves of a contraction share a span; an elision's pieces and an inversion's words have their own, D6), the module doc of `tokenize.rs`, `push_word`, `split_contraction`, `nfc_for` (French's NFC is still change 41's).
- [x] 1.6 The tests that flip at `0.2.0` are rewritten with the rule they now state (D10): in `language.rs` French reads `0.2.0`; in `tokenize.rs` the three French tests become the elision, contraction and inversion tests of 1.2–1.4; `engine.rs` `spec_scenario_a_french_page_is_read_by_the_baseline` asserts its `d'attention` as `de` + `attention` with their spans and `0.2.0`, and gains a block holding `L'homme` and `au` for `Le`/`homme` with their spans and `à` + `le`; `packs/pack.rs` loads a French pack at `0.2.0` and refuses one at `0.1.0`.
