## 0. The owner, before the implementation

Five questions, each with a recommendation, answered by the owner on 2026-10-25 (design, *Settled by
the owner*), and one consequence of the fifth answered the same day (0.6).

- [x] 0.1 [manual] **Which meaning comes first for a few common words.** A gloss opens on the dictionary's first meaning, in the dictionary's own order. A treebank of Italian school essays corrects that for small words (`fra` now opens on « Entre » before « Frère », `spesso` on « souvent » before « épais »), but for a handful it is too small, wrong, or cannot tell two entries apart, so a short reviewed list decides. Proposed rows: `gente` opens on « Gens » (people) before « Gentil, fin » (an old literary adjective); `fino` on « Jusque » (until) before « Fin » (thin); `pure` on « Aussi » (also) before « Pure »; `loro` on « Ils, elles, eux » before the polite « Loro » (the formal "you"); and `come` keeps « Comme » first (the treebank would open it on « Dès que »). Nouns, verbs and adjectives otherwise keep the dictionary's order, as for French: `piacere` stays « Plaire; Plaisir ». **Recommended: these five rows.** Add or remove one? Done 2026-10-25: the five rows as proposed — `gente`, `fino`, `pure`, `loro`, `come` (Guillaume).
- [x] 0.2 [manual] **The meaning shown for a verb form that is a word of its own.** About 1,100 words such as `assicurato` (insured), `usato` (used, second-hand) or `dovuto` (owed) have no French entry of their own, so they borrow their verb's meaning, written as an infinitive. The word card already says which verb they come from:
  ```
  assicurato
  peut aussi être le participe passé de assicurare
  [verbe] Assurer, certifier, garantir
  ```
  (a) Open the meaning on the dictionary's own words: « [verbe] (participe passé de assicurare) Assurer, certifier, garantir » — 32 characters more here, 58 on « (première personne du singulier du présent de utilizzare) Utiliser », and the card says it twice; it helps only the one-line gloss on the page. (b) Show the French word the translation tables give first, where they have one (about 320 words): `dovuto` « Dû », `impegnato` « Engagé », `utilizzo` « Utilisation », but `girato` « Tourne », `superato` « Révolu, éculé », `coinvolto` « Empêtré ». (c) Leave them as they are. **Recommended: (c)**, the card's line already explaining the form. Done 2026-10-25: (c), left as they are; the label is rejected, and no rule or requirement of this change labels a verb form's borrowed senses (Guillaume).
- [x] 0.3 [manual] **Communes and cities beside common words.** Many common words carry a small town's name as an extra meaning: `vittoria` (victory) also « Vittoria, commune et ville… » in Sicily, `lana` (wool) « Lana, commune du Pays basque », `don`, `oltre`. The rule drops a town's meaning from a common word when the dictionary calls the place a « commune » (16 words), and keeps provincial capitals (Brindisi, Foggia, Cuneo, Potenza, Matera, Prato) and cities it calls more than a commune (Cologne in `colonia`, Bordeaux, Valence). Its costs: `piacenza` loses the city of Plaisance and `cesena` the city of Cesena (keeping « Grive litorne », a bird), because the dictionary gives them no province; `trani` and `melano`, town names alone, lose their only gloss. (a) The rule, keeping Piacenza and Cesena by a short list; (b) the rule without the list; (c) also keep towns called « commune et ville » (13 words change; Vittoria, Cesena, Rho and Trani kept). **Recommended: (a).** Done 2026-10-25: (a), the commune rule, `piacenza` and `cesena` kept by a reviewed list (Guillaume); 14 words change, `trani` and `melano` lose their only gloss.
- [x] 0.4 [manual] **The wording of two corrections**, each reported to the French Wiktionary. `che` (that, which, what) has only « Quoi » and « Quel » in the dictionary, though it is mostly the relative « qui, que » and the conjunction « que ». Proposed: add both, first, so the gloss reads « Qui, que; Quoi; Que; Quel ». `dirotto` reads « Écanguer » (to break flax, its verb's meaning), while readers meet it in « piovere a dirotto » (to pour down). Proposed: « À verse (dans a dirotto) ». **Recommended: both as proposed.** (Two more fixes need no wording: `volere`'s and `io`'s example sentences, filed by the dictionary as meanings, are removed.) Done 2026-10-25: both as proposed, `che` « Qui, que; Quoi; Que; Quel » and `dirotto` « À verse (dans a dirotto) », each reported to the French Wiktionary (Guillaume).
- [x] 0.5 [manual] **Four words read as a verb where readers mean the noun.** Some Italian words are both a noun and a verb form, and the tables read them as the verb: « i dati » (the data) as *dare* (to give), « la lega » (the league) as *legare* (to tie), « una prova » (a test) as *provare* (to try). A reviewed row reads such a word as the noun instead, everywhere — so « ha dato » (has given) would then read as « dato » (a datum). Rows proposed: `dati` and `dato` → *dato*; `lega` → *lega* (its plural `leghe` comes back); `prova` → *prova*. On the held-out news treebank they add 5, 1 and 2 correctly read words of 8,909. **Recommended: all four.** Done 2026-10-25: three rows — « i dati » read as the noun, `lega` → *lega* with `leghe` back, `prova` → *prova* —, not `dato`: « mi ha dato » is the very common participle of *dare*, and reading it everywhere as the noun « donnée » would be wrong in conversation (Guillaume). How `dati` can be written without `dato` is 0.6.
- [x] 0.6 [manual] **« i dati » without « dato ».** A word of the tables has one reading, and a dictionary word exists only where its own spelling reads as it: « dati » cannot read as « dato » (the noun) while « dato » itself reads as *dare*, so the row 0.5 decided cannot be written as « dati → dato ». The nearest row: `dati` → *dati*, the plural noun as a word of its own (the English Wiktionary heads it « data »), glossed « Donnée » through `dato`'s noun; « ha dato » stays *dare*, and the rare participle « sono stati dati » reads as the noun. On the news treebank it costs one word of 8,909, since the treebank writes « dato » for the noun. **Recommended: `dati` → *dati*.** Or leave `dati` as *dare*. Done 2026-10-25: `dati` → *dati*, the plural noun as its own lemma, glossed « Données » — the plural, by a correction, since « i dati » are « les données » and the section would lend `dato`'s singular « Donnée » —; « ha dato » stays *dare*; PUD one word lower (91.35 %) accepted, the card showing « Données » instead of « Donner » (Guillaume).
